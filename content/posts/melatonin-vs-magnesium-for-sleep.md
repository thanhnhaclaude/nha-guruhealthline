Melatonin vs magnesium for sleep is a frequent question for people who want to avoid prescription sleep aids. Melatonin and magnesium are the two most popular sleep supplements. They work in completely different ways, which makes the choice easier than it looks.

## Side-by-side

| | Melatonin | Magnesium |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Hormone that signals darkness | Essential mineral |
| Best for | Falling asleep, jet lag, shift work, delayed sleep phase | Mild sleep problems, low magnesium |
| Evidence | Moderate: modestly faster sleep onset | Low quality, mainly older adults |
| Typical dose | 0.5–5 mg, 30–60 minutes before bed | 100–350 mg elemental, evening |
| Side effects | Morning grogginess, headache, vivid dreams | Loose stools at higher doses |
| Long-term data | Limited | Good safety record at normal doses |

## What the research shows

A meta-analysis of melatonin trials for primary sleep disorders found it reduced the time to fall asleep by about **7 minutes** and increased total sleep slightly. Effects are larger for **jet lag** and **delayed sleep-wake phase**.

For magnesium, a meta-analysis in older adults found low-quality evidence of a modest reduction in time to fall asleep. Benefit is most plausible in people who don't get enough magnesium.

## Choosing

- **Can't fall asleep at a normal time, or crossing time zones?** Try melatonin at a low dose.
- **Want a gentle supplement to add to a wind-down routine?** Try magnesium glycinate.
- **Children, pregnancy, or taking medications?** Talk to a doctor first, especially before melatonin.

## Safety notes

Melatonin products have been found to contain amounts different from their labels. Choose third-party tested brands. Magnesium supplements should stay under 350 mg a day and are not suitable for people with kidney disease without medical advice.

## The verdict

For sleep timing, **melatonin** has the stronger evidence. **Magnesium** is a gentle option for general sleep support, especially if your diet is low in it. Neither replaces good sleep habits or treatment for insomnia.
