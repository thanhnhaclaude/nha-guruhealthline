Creatine is one of the best-supported sports supplements. Once you decide to take it, the next question is usually which form. Creatine monohydrate vs HCl is the most common comparison.

## Side-by-side

| | Creatine monohydrate | Creatine HCl |
|---|---|---|
| Research base | Hundreds of studies | A handful of small studies |
| Typical dose | 3–5 g a day | Often 1–2 g on labels |
| Solubility in water | Moderate (micronized is better) | High |
| Cost per gram | Low | Several times higher |
| Proven to increase muscle creatine | Yes | Less data |

## Does HCl work better?

HCl's selling point is solubility: it dissolves more easily, so brands claim it absorbs better and needs a smaller dose. Better solubility in a glass of water doesn't mean more creatine reaches your muscles, and the "smaller dose" claim hasn't been confirmed in good trials.

Small studies comparing the two alongside resistance training have generally found similar improvements in strength and body composition when doses were comparable. None have shown HCl to be superior.

## Bloating and stomach upset

Creatine draws water **into muscle cells**, which adds 1–2 kg early on. That's not gut bloating. Some people do get stomach upset from large single doses of monohydrate. Fixes:

- Take 3–5 g a day instead of loading
- Split the dose and take it with meals
- Try micronized monohydrate, which mixes more easily

## The verdict

For almost everyone, **creatine monohydrate** is the better buy: proven, effective and cheap. Creatine HCl isn't harmful, but you pay more for benefits that haven't been shown.
