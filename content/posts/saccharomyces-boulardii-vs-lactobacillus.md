Saccharomyces boulardii vs Lactobacillus is a choice between two very different probiotics: one is a yeast, the other a bacterium. That difference matters for when and how you take them.

## Side-by-side

| | *S. boulardii* | *Lactobacillus* |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Yeast | Bacteria (many species and strains) |
| Killed by antibiotics | No | Yes, so space doses 2 hours apart |
| Best-studied use | Preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhea | Antibiotic diarrhea (LGG), some IBS and infant colic strains |
| Storage | Often shelf-stable | Some need refrigeration |
| Main safety caution | Immunocompromised, central lines | Immunocompromised, critically ill |

## Evidence summary

- A meta-analysis found *S. boulardii* lowered the risk of antibiotic-associated diarrhea in children and adults.
- *L. rhamnosus* GG also reduces antibiotic-associated diarrhea, especially in children.
- Other *Lactobacillus* strains are studied for IBS, infant colic and vaginal health, with mixed results that depend on the strain.

## How to choose

1. **Taking antibiotics?** *S. boulardii* is easy because you don't have to time it around doses. Details in [probiotics with antibiotics](/can-you-take-probiotics-with-antibiotics).
2. **IBS or bloating?** Look for a strain studied for your symptom and try it for 4 weeks.
3. **Check the label** for the full strain name and CFU at expiration.

## The verdict

During antibiotics, ***S. boulardii*** is the most practical choice. For other gut goals, choose a ***Lactobacillus*** strain backed by research for that specific use. Learn more in our [probiotics guide](/ingredients/probiotics).
