Cod liver oil vs fish oil is a common point of confusion, since both come from fish and both supply omega-3s. The difference is where the oil comes from, and what else comes with it.

## Side-by-side

| | Cod liver oil | Fish oil |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Liver of cod | Flesh of oily fish |
| EPA + DHA | Moderate | Moderate to high (concentrates available) |
| Vitamin A | High: about 4,080 mcg RAE per tablespoon | Little or none |
| Vitamin D | High: about 1,360 IU per tablespoon | Little or none |
| Risk of exceeding limits | Real, especially vitamin A | Low |
| Best for | People who also need vitamins A and D | Omega-3s on their own |

## The vitamin A problem

The upper limit for **preformed vitamin A** (retinol) is **3,000 mcg RAE a day** for adults. A tablespoon of cod liver oil contains more than that. Too much preformed vitamin A over time can cause:

- Liver damage
- Bone thinning and higher fracture risk
- Headaches and dizziness
- **Birth defects** when taken in high amounts during pregnancy

Capsules contain less than a tablespoon, but the amounts add up quickly, especially alongside a multivitamin.

## Vitamin D: a benefit or a risk

Cod liver oil's vitamin D can help people who get little sun. But if you already take vitamin D, check the total stays under 4,000 IU. Our guide to [how much vitamin D is too much](/how-much-vitamin-d-is-too-much) explains the limits.

## When cod liver oil makes sense

- You don't take other vitamin A or D supplements
- You eat little liver, dairy or fortified foods
- You use a modest dose, such as one teaspoon or a few capsules, and count the vitamins

## The verdict

For omega-3s, **fish oil** is the simpler, safer option: you can raise the dose without overdoing vitamins A and D. Cod liver oil can fit if you want all three nutrients, but measure carefully, and talk to your clinician first if you're pregnant. Comparing other omega-3 sources?
