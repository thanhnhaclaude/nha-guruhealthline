Vitamin C is one of the most widely taken supplements, often in doses far above what the body uses. So how much vitamin C per day do you actually need, and when is more pointless or problematic?

## Daily needs

| Group | RDA per day |
|---|---|
| Men 19+ | 90 mg |
| Women 19+ | 75 mg |
| Pregnancy (19+) | 85 mg |
| Breastfeeding (19+) | 120 mg |
| Smokers | Add 35 mg |
| Upper limit, adults | 2,000 mg |

Your body can't store much vitamin C. At intakes above a few hundred milligrams a day, absorption falls and extra is passed in the urine.

## Food sources

| Food | Serving | Vitamin C |
|---|---|---|
| Red bell pepper, raw | ½ cup | about 95 mg |
| Orange juice | ¾ cup | about 93 mg |
| Orange | 1 medium | about 70 mg |
| Kiwi | 1 medium | about 64 mg |
| Broccoli, cooked | ½ cup | about 51 mg |
| Strawberries | ½ cup | about 49 mg |

A couple of servings of fruit and vegetables covers the RDA. Cooking and long storage reduce vitamin C, so raw or lightly cooked produce is best.

## Vitamin C and colds

A large Cochrane review of placebo-controlled trials found:

- **Regular** vitamin C (at least 200 mg a day) shortened colds slightly: by about 8% in adults and 14% in children.
- It **did not reduce how often** most people caught colds.
- In people under **extreme physical stress**, such as marathon runners and soldiers in subarctic conditions, it roughly halved the risk of colds.
- Starting vitamin C **after** symptoms began showed no consistent benefit.

## Who might need more

- **Smokers** and people exposed to secondhand smoke
- People with **limited diets**, such as some older adults or people with alcohol use disorder
- People with **malabsorption** or on kidney dialysis

Severe deficiency causes **scurvy**: fatigue, gum bleeding, easy bruising and poor wound healing. It's rare in high-income countries but still occurs.

## Side effects of high doses

- Diarrhea, nausea and stomach cramps above about 2,000 mg a day
- Possible higher risk of **kidney stones** in men taking high doses long term
- People with **hemochromatosis** (iron overload) should avoid high doses, as vitamin C increases iron absorption

## The bottom line

Most adults need just **75–90 mg** of vitamin C a day, easily reached with fruit and vegetables. If you supplement, 100–500 mg covers any gap, and staying under **2,000 mg** avoids digestive side effects. Regular use may shave a little off cold duration, but it won't stop you catching one.
