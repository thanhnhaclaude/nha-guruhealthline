Vitamin D comes in two main forms. Both are converted by the liver into 25-hydroxyvitamin D, the form measured in blood tests. But in the matchup of vitamin D2 vs D3, one consistently performs better.

## Side-by-side

| | Vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol) | Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) |
|---|---|---|
| Source | UV-exposed yeast and mushrooms | Made in skin from sunlight; from lanolin or lichen in supplements |
| Raises blood levels | Yes | Yes, more effectively |
| Maintains levels | Shorter-lived | Longer-lasting |
| Vegan | Yes | Only lichen-derived D3 |
| Common in | Some prescriptions, fortified plant milks | Most supplements |

## What the research shows

A 2012 meta-analysis found D3 raised blood 25(OH)D more efficiently than D2, especially with large weekly or monthly doses. A 2024 meta-analysis of daily dosing also found D3 more effective, with the gap influenced by body weight.

Both forms do raise vitamin D levels, so D2 isn't useless. It's just less efficient.

## Which to choose

- **Most people:** vitamin D3
- **Vegans:** lichen-based D3, or D2 if that's what's available
- **Prescribed D2 by your doctor:** take it as prescribed and ask about follow-up testing

## Dose reminders

Most adults need 600–800 IU a day; many take 1,000–2,000 IU. Stay under 4,000 IU long term unless supervised. See [how much vitamin D is too much](/how-much-vitamin-d-is-too-much), and take it with a meal containing fat; see [the best time to take vitamin D](/best-time-to-take-vitamin-d).

## The verdict

**D3** is the better choice for supplements in most cases. D2 still works, particularly when prescribed, but may need higher or more frequent doses. Learn more in our [vitamin D3 guide](/ingredients/vitamin-d3).
