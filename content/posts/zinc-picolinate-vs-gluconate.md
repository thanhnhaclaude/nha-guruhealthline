Every zinc supplement uses zinc bound to another molecule. Picolinate and gluconate are two of the most common. Here's how zinc picolinate vs gluconate stacks up.

## Side-by-side

| | Zinc picolinate | Zinc gluconate |
|---|---|---|
| Bound to | Picolinic acid | Gluconic acid |
| Elemental zinc by weight | About 20% | About 14% |
| Absorption evidence | Better in one small 1987 study | Good; widely studied |
| Common format | Capsules | Tablets and cold lozenges |
| Cost | Moderate | Low |

## What the research shows

In a small crossover study of 15 adults, picolinate raised zinc in hair, urine and red blood cells more than citrate or gluconate over four weeks, while blood serum zinc didn't differ. That's the main evidence behind picolinate's reputation. It hasn't been widely replicated.

The NIH notes that gluconate, citrate and picolinate are all reasonably absorbed, while zinc oxide is absorbed less well.

## How to choose

- **Daily capsule:** picolinate or gluconate are both fine; pick by price and tolerance.
- **Cold lozenges:** acetate or gluconate.
- **Sensitive stomach:** take any form with a light meal.

## Dosing reminders

Check **elemental zinc** on the label and keep your total intake at or below **40 mg** a day. Long-term high doses cause copper deficiency. Details are in [can you take zinc every day](/can-you-take-zinc-everyday).

## The verdict

**Picolinate** has a slight edge in limited research and is a good capsule choice; **gluconate** is cheap, proven and the standard for lozenges. The dose matters more than the form. Learn more in our [zinc guide](/ingredients/zinc).
