Magnesium glycinate vs malate comes down to timing and personal preference more than chemistry. Glycinate and malate are two of the "gentle" magnesium forms. The difference between them is the molecule magnesium is attached to, and what that's marketed to do.

## Side-by-side

| | Magnesium glycinate | Magnesium malate |
|---|---|---|
| Bound to | Glycine (amino acid) | Malic acid (found in fruit) |
| Absorption | Good | Good |
| Gut tolerance | Very gentle | Gentle |
| Popular use | Evening, relaxation, sleep | Daytime, energy, muscle aches |
| Evidence for the specific claim | Limited | Limited |

## What the extras do

- **Glycine** is an amino acid with calming effects in the brain, which is why glycinate is linked with sleep. The glycine dose in a magnesium capsule is small.
- **Malic acid** takes part in the cell's energy cycle, which is why malate is marketed for fatigue. There's no strong evidence that the malic acid in a capsule boosts energy.

## How to choose

1. If you want magnesium **before bed**, glycinate is the usual choice.
2. If you prefer a **daytime** supplement, malate is a fine option.
3. If cost matters, check the **elemental magnesium** per capsule: forms differ in how much magnesium they contain.

## Dosing

Keep supplemental magnesium at or below **350 mg** a day. See [how much magnesium per day](/how-much-magnesium-per-day) for your total target, and [the best time to take magnesium](/best-time-to-take-magnesium) for timing.

## The verdict

You can't go far wrong with either. Choose **glycinate** for evenings and maximum gut comfort, **malate** for daytime use. Compare other forms in [glycinate vs citrate](/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate) and [glycinate vs oxide](/magnesium-glycinate-vs-oxide).
