Walk down any supplement aisle and you'll find a dozen kinds of magnesium. Two of the most popular are **glycinate** and **citrate**. Both are good choices, but they behave differently in your gut, and that difference is what should settle the magnesium glycinate vs citrate question for you.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Magnesium glycinate | Magnesium citrate |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Magnesium bound to the amino acid glycine | Magnesium bound to citric acid |
| Elemental magnesium | Roughly 14% by weight | Roughly 11–16% by weight |
| Absorption | Good | Good |
| Effect on digestion | Usually gentle | Mild laxative effect, stronger at higher doses |
| Typical price | Higher | Lower |
| Best for | Daily supplementing, sensitive stomachs | Occasional constipation, budget |

*Percentages vary by manufacturer. Always check the "elemental magnesium" amount on the Supplement Facts panel.*

## How magnesium glycinate works

Glycinate pairs magnesium with glycine, a small amino acid. Because the compound is absorbed in a way that leaves less unabsorbed magnesium sitting in the intestine, it draws less water into the gut. That's the main reason people with sensitive digestion tolerate it well.

Glycine itself is a calming neurotransmitter, and you'll often see glycinate marketed for sleep or anxiety. The glycine dose in a typical magnesium capsule is small, though, and good trials comparing glycinate with other forms for these outcomes are lacking. Treat the "relaxing" claim as plausible but unproven.

**Choose glycinate if:**

- You want a supplement you can take every day without thinking about it
- Other forms have given you loose stools
- You prefer taking magnesium in the evening

## How magnesium citrate works

Citrate is one of the most studied forms. In a small randomized study, magnesium citrate raised blood and urine magnesium more than magnesium oxide did. Because some of the magnesium stays in the intestine and draws in water, citrate has a gentle laxative effect. At high doses it's sold as a bowel-prep liquid for that reason.

**Choose citrate if:**

- You'd welcome some help with regularity
- You want a well-absorbed form at a lower price
- You like powders you can mix into water and dose flexibly

## Absorption: is one form clearly better?

Not by a large margin. Research consistently shows that organic magnesium salts (citrate, glycinate, aspartate, lactate) are absorbed better than magnesium oxide. Direct comparisons between citrate and glycinate are few and small, so claims that one "absorbs twice as well" as the other aren't backed by strong evidence.

What matters more in daily life is:

1. **How much elemental magnesium you actually take.** A cheaper form at the right dose beats an expensive one underdosed.
2. **Whether you can take it consistently** without stomach problems.

## Dosing glycinate and citrate

Both forms follow the same rules:

- Most adults top up with **100–200 mg of elemental magnesium** per day.
- Keep supplements at or below **350 mg per day** unless your doctor advises more.
- Split doses and take them with food if you notice any stomach upset.

## What about other forms?

- **Magnesium oxide:** high in elemental magnesium per pill but poorly absorbed. Mostly used as a laxative or antacid.
- **Magnesium L-threonate:** marketed for brain health. Evidence in humans is early and the elemental dose per capsule is low.
- **Magnesium malate and taurate:** absorbed reasonably well, with fewer studies behind them.
- **Magnesium chloride and sulfate:** often used in topical sprays and bath salts. Absorption through the skin hasn't been shown to raise magnesium levels meaningfully.

## How to pick a quality product

- Look for **third-party testing** seals (USP Verified, NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Choice).
- Check the **elemental magnesium per serving**, not just the compound weight.
- Avoid proprietary blends that hide how much of each form you're getting.
- Be wary of "buffered" glycinate if you specifically want pure glycinate. These products often add magnesium oxide to raise the elemental number.

## The verdict

There's no wrong answer between these two. Start with **glycinate** if comfort is your priority, and with **citrate** if price or regularity matters more. If one doesn't suit you, switching is easy. Keep the elemental dose and the 350 mg supplement limit in mind, and check with your doctor first if you have kidney disease.
