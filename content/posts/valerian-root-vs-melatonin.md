Valerian has been used for sleep since ancient Greece; melatonin is a modern hormone supplement. Both are sold as gentle sleep aids. Here's how valerian root vs melatonin compares on evidence and safety.

## Side-by-side

| | Valerian root | Melatonin |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Herbal root extract | Hormone that signals darkness |
| How it may work | Affects GABA, a calming brain chemical | Shifts circadian timing |
| Evidence | Mixed; some reviews positive, others not | Modest but consistent for sleep onset; strong for jet lag |
| Typical dose | 300–600 mg extract before bed | 0.5–5 mg, 30–60 minutes before bed |
| Onset | May take weeks | Same night |
| Side effects | Headache, dizziness, morning drowsiness, stomach upset | Drowsiness, headache, vivid dreams |

## What the research shows

Reviews of valerian found some studies reporting better sleep quality, but many trials were small and poorly designed, and the overall evidence is inconsistent. NCCIH notes that the research is not strong enough to say whether valerian helps.

Melatonin modestly shortens the time to fall asleep and is particularly useful for **jet lag**, **shift work** and **delayed sleep-wake phase disorder**.

## Who should avoid them

- **Pregnant or breastfeeding** people: not enough safety data for either
- **Children**: ask a pediatrician before melatonin; valerian isn't recommended
- **People taking sedatives, alcohol or anti-anxiety medicines**: added drowsiness
- **Liver problems**: rare cases of liver injury have been linked to valerian products

## The verdict

**Melatonin** is the better-supported option, especially for timing problems. **Valerian** may help some people but evidence is weak. Both are short-term aids; for ongoing insomnia, cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) has the strongest evidence.
