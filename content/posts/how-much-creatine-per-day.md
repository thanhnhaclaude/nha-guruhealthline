Creatine is one of the most researched supplements in sports nutrition, and one of the few with consistent evidence for improving strength and high-intensity performance. Working out how much creatine per day you need is simple once you understand how it works.

## How creatine works

Your muscles store creatine as **phosphocreatine**, which rapidly regenerates ATP, the energy your muscles use for short, intense efforts like lifting or sprinting. Diet (mainly meat and fish) and your body's own production keep stores around 60–80% full. Supplementing fills them up, which lets you squeeze out a few more reps or a bit more power, and over time that adds up to more strength and lean mass.

## Standard dose: 3–5 grams a day

For most adults, the evidence-based maintenance dose is **3–5 grams of creatine monohydrate per day**. At this dose:

- Muscle stores reach saturation in roughly **3–4 weeks**
- The dose stays effective with long-term daily use
- Side effects are uncommon

Bigger, more muscular people may benefit from the higher end of the range, or a little more.

### Dosing by body weight

If you prefer a body-weight approach, research often uses about **0.03 g per kg per day** for maintenance.

| Body weight | Maintenance dose (about 0.03 g/kg) |
|---|---|
| 60 kg (132 lb) | about 2 g, round up to 3 g |
| 80 kg (176 lb) | about 2.5 g, round up to 3 g |
| 100 kg (220 lb) | about 3 g |
| 120 kg (265 lb) | about 3.5–5 g |

Because 3–5 g is cheap and safe, most people simply take 5 g.

## Do you need a loading phase?

A **loading phase** means taking about **0.3 g per kg per day**, usually around **20 grams split into 4 doses**, for **5–7 days**, then dropping to 3–5 g per day.

| | Loading | No loading |
|---|---|---|
| Time to saturate muscles | About 1 week | About 3–4 weeks |
| End result | Same | Same |
| Downsides | More bloating or stomach upset for some, quicker water weight gain | Slower start |

Load if you want results quickly, for example ahead of a sports season. Otherwise, skip it.

## When to take creatine

Timing is a minor factor. A few studies suggest a small edge for taking it **close to your workout**, before or after, but results are inconsistent. What matters most:

- **Take it every day**, including rest days
- **Taking it with a meal** containing carbs and protein may slightly improve uptake and reduces stomach upset
- Mix it into water, a shake, or anything you consume daily

## Which form should you buy?

**Creatine monohydrate** is the form used in nearly all research. Other forms (HCl, buffered, ethyl ester, liquid) cost more, and none has been shown to work better. Micronized monohydrate dissolves more easily but works the same.

## Side effects and safety

- **Water weight:** Expect to gain about 1–2 kg in the first weeks, mostly water stored inside muscle. That's a sign it's working, not fat.
- **Stomach upset:** More likely with large single doses. Split doses or take with food.
- **Kidneys:** In healthy people, research hasn't shown kidney harm at recommended doses. Creatine can raise **creatinine** on blood tests, so let your doctor know you take it.
- **Hair loss:** One small 2009 study reported a rise in DHT, a hormone linked to hair loss. This hasn't been replicated or tied to actual hair loss.
- **Dehydration and cramps:** Studies don't show creatine causes these. Drink normally.

Talk to your doctor before taking creatine if you have kidney disease, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or take medication that affects the kidneys.

## The bottom line

Take **3–5 grams of creatine monohydrate every day**. Load with about 20 grams a day for a week only if you want faster results. Take it whenever it fits your routine, ideally with a meal, and stick with plain monohydrate from a tested brand.
