Omega-3 labels can be confusing. A bottle might say "1,200 mg fish oil," but what matters is how much **EPA** and **DHA** you get. Here's how much omega-3 per day makes sense for different goals.

## The three omega-3s

- **ALA** (alpha-linolenic acid): from flaxseed, chia, walnuts and canola oil. It's the only omega-3 with an official intake target.
- **EPA** (eicosapentaenoic acid) and **DHA** (docosahexaenoic acid): from oily fish and algae. These are the ones linked to heart, brain and eye health.

The body converts only a small fraction of ALA into EPA and DHA, so the advice below focuses on EPA + DHA.

## Daily amounts by goal

| Goal or group | Typical EPA + DHA per day |
|---|---|
| General health, healthy adults | 250–500 mg |
| People with coronary heart disease | About 1 g, if advised by a doctor |
| High triglycerides | 2–4 g, under medical supervision (often prescription) |
| Pregnancy and breastfeeding | Extra 200–300 mg DHA, often from 8–12 oz of low-mercury fish a week |
| Upper amount considered safe (FDA) | Up to 5 g from supplements |

### Official ALA targets

| Group | Adequate Intake (ALA) |
|---|---|
| Men | 1.6 g a day |
| Women | 1.1 g a day |
| Pregnancy | 1.4 g a day |
| Breastfeeding | 1.3 g a day |

## Food first

Two servings of oily fish a week, such as salmon, sardines, mackerel, herring or trout, provide about 250–500 mg of EPA + DHA a day on average. If you eat that much fish, you may not need a supplement.

## Reading a supplement label

1. Find the **EPA** and **DHA** amounts, usually listed separately.
2. Add them together.
3. Check the **serving size**: it may be two or three capsules.

Example: a softgel listing EPA 180 mg and DHA 120 mg gives 300 mg of EPA + DHA. Comparing fish and krill sources? See [fish oil vs krill oil](/fish-oil-vs-krill-oil).

## Safety

- **Side effects:** fishy aftertaste, burping, heartburn and loose stools. Taking omega-3 with meals helps.
- **Bleeding:** high doses may slightly increase bleeding risk, especially with blood thinners.
- **Heart rhythm:** high-dose omega-3s have been linked to a small increase in atrial fibrillation in some trials.
- **Allergies:** people with fish or shellfish allergies should talk to an allergist; algal oil is an alternative.

## The bottom line

How much omega-3 per day you need depends on your goal. For most healthy adults, **250–500 mg of EPA + DHA** from food or supplements is a sensible target.
