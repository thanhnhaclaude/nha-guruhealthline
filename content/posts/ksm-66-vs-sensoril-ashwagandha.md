KSM-66 vs Sensoril is the most common choice between branded ashwagandha extracts. Not all ashwagandha is the same. Two branded extracts appear on most premium labels: KSM-66 and Sensoril. They differ in plant parts, strength and dose.

## Side-by-side

| | KSM-66 | Sensoril |
|---|---|---|
| Plant parts | Root only | Root and leaf |
| Withanolide standardization | About 5% | At least 10% |
| Typical studied dose | 300 mg twice daily (600 mg) | 125–250 mg a day |
| Commonly studied for | Stress, anxiety, strength, testosterone | Stress, sleep, mood |
| Described effect | Calm but not sedating | More relaxing |

## What the research shows

KSM-66's best-known trial gave 300 mg twice daily for 60 days to stressed adults and found lower stress scores and lower cortisol than placebo. Sensoril trials have also reported reduced stress and better sleep at lower doses, consistent with its higher withanolide content.

There are **no head-to-head trials** comparing the two. Both are reasonable choices, and both companies fund much of their own research, which is common for branded extracts.

## How to choose

- **Prefer root-only extracts?** Choose KSM-66.
- **Want a smaller capsule and evening calm?** Sensoril is often used that way.
- **Check the label:** a product "with KSM-66" should list the KSM-66 amount, not just a blend.

## Safety applies to both

Take breaks after 2–3 months, avoid during pregnancy, and be careful with thyroid conditions and sedatives. Rare liver injury has been reported with ashwagandha. Details are in [can you take ashwagandha every day](/can-you-take-ashwagandha-every-day).

## The verdict

Both extracts are better choices than unstandardized powders. KSM-66 suits people who want root-only and daytime use; Sensoril suits those wanting a lower dose and more relaxing effect. For alternatives, see [ashwagandha vs rhodiola](/ashwagandha-vs-rhodiola).
