Zinc is needed every day because your body has no dedicated zinc store. That makes a daily supplement reasonable for some people. The question is how much, and for how long, you can take zinc every day before it causes problems.

## How much you need

| Group | RDA |
|---|---|
| Men 19+ | 11 mg |
| Women 19+ | 8 mg |
| Pregnancy | 11 mg |
| Breastfeeding | 12 mg |
| **Upper limit (all sources)** | **40 mg** |

Most people in high-income countries get 8–15 mg from food. Meat, shellfish (especially oysters), dairy, beans, nuts and whole grains are the main sources.

## Is a daily zinc supplement safe?

At **15–25 mg a day**, a daily supplement keeps most people well within the upper limit even after adding food. Problems come with doses above 40 mg for weeks to months:

- **Copper deficiency:** zinc triggers a protein in gut cells that traps copper. Over time this can cause anemia, low white blood cells and nerve damage, sometimes permanent.
- **Lower HDL ("good") cholesterol**
- **Weaker immune function**, the opposite of what most people want from zinc

Dental adhesive creams containing zinc have caused copper deficiency when used heavily, so count them too.

## Who might benefit from daily zinc

- **Vegetarians and vegans**: plant phytates reduce absorption, so needs may be up to 50% higher
- People with **digestive diseases** or after **weight-loss surgery**
- **Older adults** with limited diets
- People with **heavy alcohol use**

## Zinc lozenges are different

Cold lozenges deliver high doses (around 75 mg a day) for a short time. They should be used for **no more than about a week**, starting within 24 hours of symptoms. They are not meant for daily long-term use. Avoid nasal zinc sprays, which have been linked to permanent loss of smell.

## How to take zinc every day

1. **Pick a moderate dose:** 15–25 mg of elemental zinc.
2. **Take it with a light meal** if it upsets your stomach.
3. **Space it from iron** and high-dose calcium supplements.
4. **Separate from antibiotics:** take tetracyclines or quinolones 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after zinc.
5. **Consider copper** if you take more than 25 mg for months.

Combining it with magnesium is fine; see [magnesium and zinc together](/can-you-take-magnesium-and-zinc-together).

## The bottom line

You can take zinc every day if your total intake stays at or below **40 mg**. A moderate 15–25 mg supplement suits people at risk of low zinc; higher doses should be short-term and supervised. For forms, benefits and evidence grades, see our [zinc guide](/ingredients/zinc).
