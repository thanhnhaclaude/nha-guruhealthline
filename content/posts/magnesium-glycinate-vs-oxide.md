Magnesium glycinate vs oxide is really a question of what you want magnesium to do. Magnesium oxide is in many multivitamins and cheap tablets. Magnesium glycinate costs more. Is it worth it? It depends on why you're taking magnesium.

## Side-by-side

| | Magnesium glycinate | Magnesium oxide |
|---|---|---|
| Elemental magnesium by weight | About 14% | About 60% |
| Absorption | Good | Poor |
| Laxative effect | Minimal | Strong at higher doses |
| Main use | Raising magnesium intake | Constipation, heartburn |
| Cost | Higher | Very low |

## Why oxide absorbs poorly

Oxide is an inorganic salt that doesn't dissolve well in the gut. Studies comparing forms, including a randomized trial, found organic forms such as citrate raised blood and urine magnesium more than oxide. The magnesium that isn't absorbed pulls water into the intestine, which is why oxide works as a laxative.

## When oxide makes sense

- Occasional **constipation**
- **Heartburn** relief as an antacid
- A very low budget, accepting that less is absorbed

## When glycinate makes sense

- Daily supplementing to close a dietary gap
- Sensitive stomach
- Evening use without bathroom trips

## Dosing and safety

Supplemental magnesium of any form should stay at or below **350 mg** a day unless your doctor advises more. Separate magnesium from some antibiotics, bisphosphonates and thyroid medication. See [the best time to take magnesium](/best-time-to-take-magnesium).

## The verdict

Choose **glycinate** if you want to raise your magnesium. Choose **oxide** only if constipation relief is the goal. For another comparison, read [magnesium glycinate vs citrate](/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate).
