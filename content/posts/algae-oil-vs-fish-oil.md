Fish don't make omega-3s themselves. They get them by eating algae, or eating smaller fish that ate algae. Algae oil skips the middleman. So when it comes to algae oil vs fish oil, the question is mostly about dose, diet and price.

## Side-by-side

| | Algae oil | Fish oil |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Cultivated microalgae | Anchovy, sardine, mackerel and other oily fish |
| Main omega-3 | Mostly DHA; some products add EPA | EPA and DHA, often more EPA |
| Suitable for vegans | Yes | No |
| Contaminant risk | Very low (grown in controlled tanks) | Low after purification; depends on testing |
| Fishy aftertaste | Rare | Common |
| Cost per mg EPA + DHA | Higher | Lower |
| Research base | Smaller, growing | Large |

## Is algal DHA as good?

A study comparing algal-oil capsules with cooked salmon found they raised blood DHA levels equally. Your body handles DHA the same way regardless of where it came from.

The main difference is **EPA**. Many algae oils contain mostly DHA. Much of the research on triglycerides and mood has focused on EPA or EPA + DHA combinations, which is where fish oil has an edge.

## Who should choose algae oil

- Vegans and vegetarians
- People who dislike fishy burps
- Pregnant or breastfeeding people who want DHA without mercury concerns
- People with fish allergies (ask your allergist first)

## Who should choose fish oil

- People wanting **higher EPA** doses
- Anyone on a budget
- People following studies that used fish oil specifically

## Dose

For general health, aim for **250–500 mg of combined EPA + DHA** a day. Details by goal are in [how much omega-3 per day](/how-much-omega-3-per-day).

## The verdict

Algae oil is a clean, vegan source of DHA that works as well as fish for raising DHA levels. Fish oil delivers more EPA for less money. Choose by diet and by whether you need EPA. Comparing fish-based options? See [fish oil vs krill oil](/fish-oil-vs-krill-oil).
