Many adults eat less magnesium than recommended, but true magnesium deficiency is less common, because healthy kidneys hold on to magnesium when intake is low. When magnesium deficiency symptoms do appear, they usually progress in stages.

## Early signs

- Loss of appetite
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fatigue
- Weakness

These are vague and have many possible causes, which is why deficiency is easy to miss.

## Signs of more severe deficiency

- **Numbness and tingling**
- **Muscle cramps, twitches and spasms**
- **Personality changes**
- **Abnormal heart rhythms**
- **Seizures**
- **Coronary spasms**

Severe deficiency also disturbs other minerals, leading to **low calcium** and **low potassium** levels, which add their own symptoms.

## Who's at risk

| Risk group | Why |
|---|---|
| Digestive diseases (Crohn's, celiac, chronic diarrhea, gut surgery) | Less magnesium is absorbed and more is lost |
| Type 2 diabetes | High blood sugar increases magnesium loss in urine |
| Long-term heavy alcohol use | Poor intake, vomiting and increased urinary loss |
| Older adults | Absorb less and lose more; more likely to take interacting medicines |
| Long-term proton pump inhibitors (e.g. omeprazole) | Can lower magnesium after months of use |
| Loop and thiazide diuretics | Increase magnesium loss in urine |

## How it's diagnosed

Doctors usually start with a **serum magnesium** blood test. Because most magnesium is stored in bone and cells, a normal result doesn't always rule out low body stores. Your doctor may also check calcium and potassium, and review your medications and medical history.

## Getting enough magnesium

For most people, food comes first: pumpkin seeds, almonds, spinach, black beans and whole grains are good sources. If you need a supplement:

- Most adults top up with **100–200 mg** of elemental magnesium a day
- Supplements alone shouldn't exceed **350 mg** a day unless prescribed
- Gentle forms such as glycinate cause fewer stomach problems; see [glycinate vs citrate](/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate)

Your full daily target by age and sex is in our guide to [how much magnesium per day](/how-much-magnesium-per-day).

> **Kidney disease:** if your kidneys don't work well, you can build up too much magnesium. Don't supplement without medical advice.

## The bottom line

Magnesium deficiency symptoms start subtly, with tiredness, weakness, nausea and poor appetite, and only later bring cramps, tingling and heart rhythm problems. If you have symptoms and a risk factor such as diabetes, a digestive condition or long-term PPI use, ask your doctor about testing before self-treating.
