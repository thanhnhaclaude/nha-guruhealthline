Berberine is a yellow plant compound found in goldenseal, barberry and Oregon grape. It has become popular for blood sugar and cholesterol support. If you're considering it for more than a few weeks, the key question is: can you take berberine long-term?

## What the studies used

Most clinical trials gave:

- **900–1,500 mg per day**, split into two or three doses with meals
- For **8 weeks to 6 months**, with only a few longer studies

Within this window, berberine lowered fasting blood sugar and LDL cholesterol modestly in people with type 2 diabetes or high cholesterol. Benefits for weight loss have been small.

## What we don't know

Long-term safety data, beyond about six months, are limited. Supplements also vary widely in purity and actual berberine content. That's a reason to use berberine with medical oversight if you plan to take it for many months.

## Common side effects

Digestive side effects are the most frequent:

- Constipation or diarrhea
- Cramping and bloating
- Nausea

Splitting the daily dose and taking it with meals usually helps.

## Drug interactions: the main concern

Berberine affects liver enzymes (CYP3A4 and CYP2D6) and drug transporters that process many medicines. It may raise or lower levels of other drugs. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before combining it with:

| Medication type | Concern |
|---|---|
| Diabetes medicines (metformin, insulin, sulfonylureas) | Added blood-sugar lowering; risk of hypoglycemia |
| Cyclosporine and other transplant drugs | Changes in drug levels |
| Blood thinners | Possible changes in effect |
| Sedatives | May increase drowsiness |
| Drugs metabolized by CYP3A4 or CYP2D6 | Altered drug levels |

Our [interaction checker](/tools/supplement-interaction-checker) covers common supplement combinations, but berberine's drug interactions need a pharmacist's review. Watch for symptoms of low blood sugar: shakiness, sweating, fast heartbeat, confusion and hunger.

## Who should avoid berberine

- **Pregnant or breastfeeding** people: berberine can pass to the baby and may cause jaundice-related brain damage (kernicterus) in newborns
- **Newborns and infants**
- People with **liver disease**, unless supervised
- Anyone taking several prescription medicines without checking interactions first

## If you take it for months

1. Agree a plan with your doctor, especially if you have diabetes.
2. Recheck blood sugar (and HbA1c) and, if advised, liver tests.
3. Use a **third-party tested** product.
4. Reassess every few months whether it's still needed.

## The bottom line

Berberine has been studied for up to about six months at 900–1,500 mg a day and was generally well tolerated apart from digestive upset. Taking berberine long term isn't well studied, and its many drug interactions make medical guidance important, especially alongside diabetes medication. Avoid it in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
