Ashwagandha is usually taken daily, and that's how it was studied. The real question isn't whether you can take ashwagandha every day, but for how long, and what to watch for along the way.

## What the research used

Clinical trials of ashwagandha root extract typically gave:

- **300–600 mg per day**, often split into two doses
- **Every day for 6–12 weeks**
- Standardized extracts, most commonly root-only

Within these limits, side effects were usually mild: drowsiness, stomach upset and loose stools.

## What we don't know

Very few studies have followed people for longer than about three months. That doesn't mean longer use is harmful; it means the safety data are thin. The NIH describes ashwagandha as possibly safe for short-term use, with less certainty beyond that.

## Should you take breaks?

There's no clinical trial comparing continuous use with cycling. Still, many clinicians suggest a practical pattern:

1. Take it daily for **8–12 weeks**.
2. Pause for **2–4 weeks**.
3. Reassess. If stress or sleep improved and stays better off it, you may not need to restart.

This keeps your use close to what's been studied and gives you a chance to judge whether it's actually helping.

## Warning signs: when to stop

The most important rare risk is **liver injury**. NIH's LiverTox database documents cases linked to ashwagandha, usually appearing within the first few months. Stop taking it and contact a doctor if you notice:

- Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice)
- Dark urine or pale stools
- Itching all over
- Unusual fatigue, nausea or pain in the upper right abdomen

## Who shouldn't take it daily

- **Pregnant or breastfeeding** people
- People with **hyperthyroidism** or who take thyroid medication
- People with **autoimmune diseases** such as lupus or rheumatoid arthritis
- People with **hormone-sensitive prostate cancer**
- Anyone scheduled for **surgery**: stop about two weeks before, since it may add to sedation

Ashwagandha may also interact with sedatives, immunosuppressants, and medicines for diabetes, blood pressure and thyroid disease. Check with your pharmacist or use our [interaction checker](/tools/supplement-interaction-checker) as a starting point.

## The bottom line

Taking ashwagandha every day is how it was studied, and short-term daily use at 300–600 mg is generally well tolerated. Because long-term safety isn't established, consider a break after two to three months, and stop immediately if you see signs of liver problems.
