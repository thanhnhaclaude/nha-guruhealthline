Your brain's ability to focus and remember depends on how well it's rested, fueled and challenged. These eight habits have the strongest support.

## 1. Protect your sleep

Sleep consolidates memories. Aim for 7–9 hours with a regular schedule. See [how to sleep better naturally](/articles/how-to-sleep-better-naturally/).

## 2. Move your body

Regular aerobic exercise is linked to better memory and attention. Even a brisk 20-minute walk helps focus afterward.

## 3. Single-task

Switching tasks costs attention. Silence notifications and work in focused 25–50 minute blocks.

## 4. Use spaced repetition

Review information over increasing intervals instead of cramming. Testing yourself beats rereading.

## 5. Manage stress

Chronic stress impairs memory. Breathing exercises, time outdoors and social connection help.

## 6. Eat for steady energy

Regular meals with protein and fiber avoid energy dips. Stay hydrated.

## 7. Keep learning

New skills, languages and social activities challenge the brain.

## 8. Check for medical causes

Low B12 or iron, thyroid problems, depression, hearing loss and some medications affect memory and focus.

## What about supplements?

Caffeine with L-theanine improves short-term attention. Other "brain boosters" have weak evidence. See our [brain health guide](/brain-health/) and [brain fog guide](/supplements-for-brain-fog-and-focus/).
