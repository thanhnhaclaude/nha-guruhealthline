Ashwagandha is mostly studied for stress, but sleep is often the reason people try it. Here's what we know about using ashwagandha for sleep.

## What the research shows

A 2021 meta-analysis pooled five randomized trials with about 400 adults. Ashwagandha produced a small but statistically significant improvement in sleep compared with placebo. Benefits were larger in people diagnosed with insomnia, at doses of at least 600 mg a day, and when taken for 8 weeks or more. Effects on daytime alertness and anxiety also improved slightly.

## How it might work

Ashwagandha may lower stress hormones such as cortisol and affect GABA signaling, which plays a role in relaxation. Improved sleep may partly follow from lower stress.

## Dosing used in trials

| | Typical in studies |
|---|---|
| Dose | 300–600 mg root extract a day |
| Timing | Split morning and evening, or evening only |
| Duration | 6–12 weeks |

## Safety

- Avoid during pregnancy and breastfeeding
- Be cautious with thyroid conditions, autoimmune disease and sedative medicines
- Rare liver injury has been reported; stop if you notice yellow skin or dark urine

Read [can you take ashwagandha every day](/can-you-take-ashwagandha-every-day/) for long-term use.

## How it compares

For falling asleep faster or jet lag, melatonin has stronger evidence. For stress-related poor sleep, ashwagandha is a reasonable option. See our [sleep supplements guide](/sleep/) and [melatonin vs magnesium](/melatonin-vs-magnesium-for-sleep/).
