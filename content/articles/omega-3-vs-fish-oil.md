"Omega-3" and "fish oil" are often used interchangeably, but they're not the same thing.

## The three main omega-3s

| Omega-3 | Main sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ALA | Flaxseed, chia, walnuts, canola oil | Essential; converts poorly to EPA/DHA |
| EPA | Oily fish, fish oil, some algae oils | Linked to triglyceride and mood research |
| DHA | Oily fish, fish oil, algae oil | Key for brain and eyes |

## Reading a fish oil label

Look for **EPA** and **DHA** amounts. "1,000 mg fish oil" with "EPA 180 mg, DHA 120 mg" gives 300 mg of omega-3s per capsule. Concentrated products may provide 600–900 mg.

## Omega-3 sources compared

- **Fish oil:** most research, best value
- **Krill oil:** smaller capsules, lower dose; see [fish oil vs krill oil](/fish-oil-vs-krill-oil/)
- **Algae oil:** vegan DHA; see [algae oil vs fish oil](/algae-oil-vs-fish-oil/)
- **Cod liver oil:** adds vitamins A and D; see [cod liver oil vs fish oil](/cod-liver-oil-vs-fish-oil/)

## How much?

Most adults do well with 250–500 mg of EPA + DHA daily. Read [how much omega-3 per day](/how-much-omega-3-per-day/).
