Zinc is a trace mineral, which means you need only a little of it, but it's involved in hundreds of processes: immune function, wound healing, DNA production, taste and smell. Your body can't store much zinc, so you need a steady supply from your diet, and sometimes from a zinc supplement.

## What zinc does in the body

- **Immune defense:** Immune cells need zinc to develop and work properly. Even mild deficiency can weaken your response to infection.
- **Wound healing:** Zinc supports skin repair and collagen production.
- **Growth and development:** It's essential during pregnancy, childhood and adolescence.
- **Taste and smell:** Low zinc can dull both senses.
- **Enzymes:** More than 300 enzymes rely on zinc to function.

## How much zinc do you need?

| Group | Recommended daily intake (RDA) |
|---|---|
| Men 19+ | 11 mg |
| Women 19+ | 8 mg |
| Pregnancy (19+) | 11 mg |
| Breastfeeding (19+) | 12 mg |
| Upper limit, adults (all sources) | 40 mg |

Unlike [magnesium, whose limit covers supplements only](/how-much-magnesium-per-day), zinc's upper limit applies to **food and supplements combined**.

## Who might need a zinc supplement?

Most people in high-income countries get enough zinc from food. You're more likely to run low if you:

- **Eat a vegetarian or vegan diet.** Plant foods contain phytates that reduce zinc absorption, so needs may be up to 50% higher.
- **Have a digestive disorder** such as Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis or celiac disease, or have had weight-loss surgery
- **Drink alcohol heavily**
- **Are pregnant or breastfeeding** and eat few zinc-rich foods
- **Are an older adult** with a limited diet
- **Have sickle cell disease**

## Best food sources

| Food | Serving | Zinc |
|---|---|---|
| Oysters, cooked | 3 oz | about 32 mg |
| Beef, cooked | 3 oz | about 4–5 mg |
| Crab, cooked | 3 oz | about 3 mg |
| Fortified breakfast cereal | 1 serving | about 3 mg (varies) |
| Pumpkin seeds | 1 oz | about 2 mg |
| Pork or chicken, cooked | 3 oz | about 2–3 mg |
| Chickpeas or lentils, cooked | ½ cup | about 1–1.5 mg |

## Forms of zinc supplements

| Form | Notes |
|---|---|
| Zinc picolinate | Well absorbed in a small study; common in capsules |
| Zinc gluconate | Widely used, including in lozenges |
| Zinc citrate | Absorbed about as well as gluconate, less bitter |
| Zinc acetate | Common in cold lozenges |
| Zinc oxide | Cheap, absorbed less well |

Check the **elemental zinc** on the label. "50 mg zinc gluconate" contains only about 7 mg of zinc, while a label stating "Zinc (as picolinate) 25 mg" refers to elemental zinc.

## Zinc for colds

Zinc lozenges have been studied for decades. Some meta-analyses found that lozenges providing about 75 mg of zinc per day, started within 24 hours of symptoms, shortened colds by roughly a third. Other reviews found smaller or inconsistent effects. If you try them:

- Start **at the first sign** of a cold
- Use them for **no more than about a week**
- **Avoid nasal zinc sprays and gels.** They have been linked to permanent loss of smell.

## Side effects and risks

**Short term, high doses** can cause nausea, vomiting, stomach cramps, diarrhea and a metallic taste.

**Long term, above 40 mg a day,** zinc can:

- Cause **copper deficiency**, leading to anemia and nerve damage
- Lower "good" HDL cholesterol
- Weaken immune function, the opposite of what most people want

### Medication interactions

- **Antibiotics** (quinolones and tetracyclines): zinc reduces absorption of both. Take the antibiotic at least 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after zinc.
- **Penicillamine** (used for rheumatoid arthritis and Wilson's disease): zinc reduces its effect.
- **Thiazide diuretics:** increase zinc loss in urine.

## The bottom line

Most adults get the zinc they need, 8–11 mg a day, from food. A supplement makes sense if you're at higher risk of deficiency. Choose a moderate dose (15–25 mg), keep total intake under **40 mg per day**, and don't take high doses for months without medical advice. For colds, lozenges started early may help a little; nasal zinc should be avoided.
