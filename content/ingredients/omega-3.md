There are three main omega-3s. **ALA** comes from plants such as flax and walnuts. **EPA** and **DHA** come mainly from fish and algae, and they're the ones linked to most health benefits. The body converts only a small fraction of ALA into EPA and DHA, so supplements focus on the latter two.

## Sources compared

| Source | EPA + DHA per capsule | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrated fish oil | 500–900 mg | Best value per mg |
| Standard fish oil | about 300 mg | Large capsules |
| Krill oil | 100–250 mg | Small capsules, higher cost |
| Algal oil | 200–500 mg (mostly DHA) | Vegan option |
| Cod liver oil | about 200 mg | Also contains vitamins A and D |

We compare the two most common options in [fish oil vs krill oil](/fish-oil-vs-krill-oil).

## How to choose a good product

- Read **EPA + DHA per serving**, not "total fish oil"
- Look for third-party purity testing (IFOS, USP or NSF)
- Choose sustainably sourced oil (MSC or Friend of the Sea)
- Avoid oil that smells strongly rancid

## Safety

Common side effects are fishy burps, heartburn and loose stools; taking omega-3s with food helps. High doses may slightly increase bleeding risk with anticoagulants, and trials have linked high-dose omega-3s to a small rise in atrial fibrillation in people with heart risk factors. Ask your doctor before taking more than 2 g a day.
