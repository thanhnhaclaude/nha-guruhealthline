Ashwagandha has been used in Ayurvedic medicine for centuries and is now one of the best-selling herbal supplements. It's called an "adaptogen," a term for herbs thought to help the body handle stress, though that's a traditional idea rather than a defined medical category.

## What the research shows

Most human trials used root extracts for 6–12 weeks in adults reporting stress. Several found lower stress and anxiety scores and, in some, lower cortisol compared with placebo. The trials are generally small and short, and some were funded by extract makers, so the evidence is promising but not definitive.

## Choosing a product

- Prefer **root extracts** with the withanolide content listed
- Branded extracts such as KSM-66 and Sensoril have their own trials, at different doses
- Look for third-party testing for purity

If you're deciding between adaptogens, see [ashwagandha vs rhodiola](/ashwagandha-vs-rhodiola).

## Side effects and who should avoid it

Common side effects include drowsiness and stomach upset. Rare cases of liver injury have been reported; stop and see a doctor if you notice yellow skin or eyes or dark urine. Avoid it during pregnancy and breastfeeding, and talk to a doctor first if you have a thyroid or autoimmune condition, or take sedatives, thyroid medication, immunosuppressants, or diabetes or blood pressure drugs.
