L-theanine gives tea its savory taste and is part of why a cup of tea feels calmer than a cup of coffee with the same caffeine. As a supplement it's used for stress, focus and sleep.

## How it works

L-theanine crosses into the brain and influences calming and alerting chemical messengers, including GABA, serotonin and dopamine. EEG studies show more **alpha brain wave** activity, a pattern linked to relaxed alertness.

## L-theanine with caffeine

The combination is popular because L-theanine may smooth out caffeine's jittery edge while keeping its alertness. Trials typically used about **2:1 theanine to caffeine**, such as 200 mg L-theanine with 100 mg caffeine, and found better accuracy and attention on demanding tasks.

## How much to take

| Goal | Typical dose |
|---|---|
| Stress, calm focus | 200 mg, once or twice a day |
| Sleep quality | 200–400 mg about an hour before bed |
| With caffeine | About twice the caffeine dose |

Green tea contains roughly 8–30 mg per cup, so supplements deliver much more than tea.

## Safety

L-theanine was well tolerated in studies, with few side effects. Talk to a doctor before use if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, take blood pressure medication (it may lower blood pressure slightly) or take sedatives or stimulants.
