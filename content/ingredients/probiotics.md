Your gut hosts trillions of microbes that help digest food, train the immune system and keep harmful organisms in check. Probiotic supplements aim to add helpful microbes. They work best for specific problems, not as a general wellness pill.

## Strain matters most

A probiotic's name has three parts: **genus**, **species** and **strain**, for example *Lactobacillus rhamnosus* GG. Two products with the same species but different strains can behave differently. Look for products that list the full strain name and match it to research.

## Uses with the best evidence

- **Antibiotic-associated diarrhea:** see [probiotics with antibiotics](/can-you-take-probiotics-with-antibiotics) for timing and strains.
- **Some IBS symptoms:** certain strains may reduce bloating and pain for some people; it's worth a 4-week trial.
- **Infant colic:** some evidence for *L. reuteri* in breastfed babies.

## Reading the label

| Label item | What to check |
|---|---|
| Strain | Full name, e.g. *Bifidobacterium animalis* subsp. *lactis* BB-12 |
| CFU | Count **at expiration**, not at manufacture |
| Storage | Refrigerate if instructed |
| Testing | Third-party verification of strain and count |

Higher CFU isn't automatically better; the dose that worked in trials is the best guide.

## Who should be careful

Probiotics are safe for most healthy people. Mild gas and bloating are common in the first days. Rare serious infections have occurred in people who are **critically ill**, **immunocompromised**, have a **central line**, or are **premature infants**. Talk to a doctor first in these cases.
