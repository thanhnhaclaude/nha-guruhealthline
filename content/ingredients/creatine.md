Creatine is made naturally in your liver, kidneys and pancreas and stored mostly in muscle. Meat and fish supply about 1–2 grams a day. Supplementing tops up muscle stores so you can produce a little more power on hard efforts, which adds up over weeks of training.

## Who benefits most

- People doing resistance training or sprint-type sports
- Vegetarians and vegans, who start with lower muscle stores
- Older adults combining it with strength training to limit age-related muscle loss

## How to use it

Take 3–5 grams of creatine monohydrate every day. A loading phase is optional and only speeds up the first week. Our [creatine dosing guide](/how-much-creatine-per-day) covers loading, timing and dosing by body weight, and the [creatine calculator](/tools/creatine-calculator) does the math for you.

## Safety

In healthy people, long-term use at recommended doses hasn't been shown to harm the kidneys or liver. Expect 1–2 kg of water weight in muscle at first. Creatine can raise creatinine on blood tests without indicating kidney damage, so tell your doctor you take it. People with kidney disease should ask a doctor first.
